## Description

`docs/deployment.md`'s restore drill instructs, at step 4b, that the drill's
source database be seeded **one migration behind HEAD** before step 1 — so the
drill exercises a real pending migration, which is the whole point after
`#0235`.

**Step 1 makes that impossible.** It is `scripts/testdb.sh create`, whose
`check_template_fresh` *guarantees* the template is at HEAD. Follow the drill
literally and there is nothing pending: step 4b silently no-ops, and the drill
reports success having proved exactly the thing `#0235` exists to catch.

Found by `#0064`/`#0235`'s phase-3 review, which had to **deviate from step 1**
to reproduce `#0235`'s bug at all.

**Why this is worse than a wording bug.** The drill's own prose warns against
the outcome it now produces. A drill that passes while testing nothing is
strictly worse than no drill, because it manufactures confidence — and the
thing it fails to test is whether the app can migrate after a disaster restore,
which is the moment nobody wants a surprise.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The drill is followable start to finish, in order, with no step contradicting an earlier one
- [ ] It produces a genuinely pending migration — by not using the at-HEAD template, or by rolling one back after creating it, whichever is simpler to keep true
- [ ] Someone other than the author follows it verbatim on a scratch database and reaches a real `migrate up` that actually applies something; `## Verification` names who and what it printed
- [ ] The drill fails loudly if nothing was pending, rather than passing quietly — the failure mode being fixed is a silent no-op
- [ ] `#0235`'s reproduction is reachable by following the drill rather than by deviating from it
