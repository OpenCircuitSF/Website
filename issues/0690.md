## Description

That set does not exist on main yet, and an empty set fails `guideSets.test.ts` and `GuidesIndex.behavior.test.ts`, so **each of the three issues adds the same set entry** (appended last in `sets`, exactly as below) and updates those two tests' expected set lists. Audience: makers at an Open Circuit SF workshop, mostly beginners, building with an ESP32-C3 SuperMini (3.3 V logic, about 20–40 mA per pin at most and less is better) and a USB 5 V supply. Use those numbers in the examples.

Guides in this issue (slugs may be adjusted):

- `resistor-color-codes` — reading resistor colour bands. Demo: a resistor drawing with 4- and 5-band modes; tap a band to cycle its colour, or type a value and see the bands. Shows value, tolerance, and the nearest E12/E24 value.
- `ohms-law-resistors` — Ohm's law, series and parallel, and power rating. Demo: a small circuit with a source and one or two resistors (series/parallel toggle); sliders for voltage and resistance show current and the power in each resistor, warning when it exceeds a 1/4 W part.
- `led-basics` — LED polarity (long leg, flat edge), forward voltage by colour, and why an LED needs a resistor. Demo: an LED drawing where the reader flips the LED around and picks a colour; a glowing or dark LED and the current flowing or not.
- `led-resistor-calculator` — picking the current-limiting resistor. Demo: supply (3.3 V / 5 V / custom), LED colour (Vf), target current; shows the exact R, the next E12 value up, the resulting current and resistor power, and a warning for an ESP32 pin above the safe current.

All logic is in `web/src/lib/topics/resistorsLeds.ts` (colour code, `bandsFor`/`readBands`, `parseOhms`, E12/E24 `nearestE`/`nextEUp`, `solve` for Ohm’s law, `ledState`, `ledResistor`) with 28 unit tests in `resistorsLeds.test.ts`. Each demo has one jsdom behaviour test in `demos.smoke.test.ts`. Controls are native buttons, radios-as-buttons with `aria-checked`, checkboxes, range and number inputs with labels; the SVG band buttons draw an `--accent` focus stroke. Colours use theme tokens (`--accent`, `--danger`, `--text`…), except the physical drawings (band colours, LED light, resistor body), which are literal.

The SVGs were checked by dumping each demo’s rendered SVG from jsdom and rendering it with `rsvg-convert` (no browser); that caught and fixed overlapping labels in the parallel schematic and the 5-band labels.

Facts used (standard):
- IEC 60062 colour code: digits black 0 … white 9; multiplier black ×1 … white ×1G, gold ×0.1, silver ×0.01; tolerance brown 1%, red 2%, green 0.5%, blue 0.25%, violet 0.1%, gold 5%, silver 10%. 4-band = 2 digits, 5-band = 3 digits.
- IEC 60063 E12 (10 12 15 18 22 27 33 39 47 56 68 82) and E24 (adds 11 13 16 20 24 30 36 43 51 62 75 91).
- Ohm’s law, series/parallel, P = V × I; ordinary through-hole resistors are 1/4 W.

### Assumptions
- LED forward voltages are typical round values from memory of common 5 mm LED datasheets, not one specific part: red 2.0 V, yellow 2.1 V, green 2.2 V (ordinary GaP-type green), blue and white 3.2 V. The guide text says bright “true green” LEDs need about 3 V.
- 20 mA as the continuous maximum for a plain 5 mm indicator LED (typical datasheet figure).
- ESP32-C3 pin current: about 20 mA comfortably (the default drive strength) and about 40 mA at most, from memory of the ESP32-C3 datasheet; the demo warns above 20 mA from a pin and suggests 5–10 mA.
- The LED model is the usual simplified one (no current until the supply exceeds Vf, then (Vs − Vf)/R). Real blue/white LEDs on 3.3 V often glow a little more than it predicts; the text says “how dimly varies from LED to LED”.
- Tolerance cycling only offers the common tolerance colours; the newer IEC 60062:2016 orange/yellow/grey tolerance codes are left out.
- A reversed LED on 3.3 V is described as harmless (typical reverse-voltage rating is 5 V).
- Spelling follows the site’s American “color” in reader-facing text.

## Review notes

Reviewed `git diff main...HEAD` (2026-11-16). Verdict: **resolved**, with three small fixes applied in review.

Checked against the standards and by hand:
- IEC 60062 colour code: digits black 0 to white 9; multiplier black x1 to white x1G, gold x0.1, silver x0.01; tolerances brown 1%, red 2%, green 0.5%, blue 0.25%, violet 0.1%, gold 5%, silver 10%. 5-band = 3 digits + multiplier + tolerance. First band excludes black. All correct.
- `bandsFor` handles carry (995 on 4 bands becomes 1.0 kΩ, flagged inexact) and range (gold/silver for sub-10 Ω, null below 0.1 Ω or past white). `parseOhms` handles 4k7, 4R7, 1M (mega), 10 kΩ, 470 ohms.
- E12/E24 tables match IEC 60063; `nearestE` is by ratio; `nextEUp` rounds up, so `ledResistor` never exceeds the target current.
- Worked numbers in the text: 220 Ω ±5% = 209–231 Ω; 5 V/220 Ω = 22.7 mA, 114 mW; 5 V/47 Ω = 532 mW; (3.3 − 2.0)/10 mA = 130 Ω → 150 Ω → 8.67 mA. All correct.
- LED polarity: long leg = anode toward the pin, flat rim edge on the cathode side; the drawing keeps both right when flipped. Forward voltages and the ESP32-C3 20/40 mA figures are listed under Assumptions.
- Every band has a text label (digit/multiplier/tolerance and the colour name) under it and in its `aria-label`, so colour is never the only channel. The LED calculator lists its band colours as text. Controls are real buttons, radios, checkboxes and labelled inputs; the SVG band buttons draw an `--accent` focus stroke and the rest pick up `app.css`'s global `--accent` ring. Fixed physical colours (beige/blue body, grey leads) sit on their own body and read on both grounds; labels use theme tokens.

Fixes made in review:
1. `LedResistorDemo.svelte`: the power warning said "use a 1/2 W part" even above 0.5 W (custom 24 V, red, 30 mA gives about 0.59 W). It now says 1 W above 0.5 W.
2. `LedBasicsDemo.svelte`: the "burnt" no-resistor dome was drawn at 0.3 opacity because the inline `fill-opacity` overrode the class, so it nearly disappeared on the dark theme. The inline opacity is now dropped in that state. The glow gradient id uses `$props.id()`, as `ResistorBandsDemo` already did, so it cannot clash.
3. `ResistorBandsDemo.svelte`: switching 5 → 4 bands rounded a 3-digit value (for example 4.99 kΩ to 5.0 kΩ) and switching 4 → 5 on a value below 1 Ω reset it to 220 Ω, both silently. The note line now says what happened.
