## Description

On 2026-11-16 the user asked for guides on common electrical components (resistors, capacitors, transistors, MOSFETs and LEDs), noting that "interactive diagrams are very helpful". That set does not exist on main yet, and an empty set fails `guideSets.test.ts` and `GuidesIndex.behavior.test.ts`, so **each of the three issues adds the same set entry** (appended last in `sets`, exactly as below) and updates those two tests' expected set lists. Audience: makers at an Open Circuit SF workshop, mostly beginners, building with an ESP32-C3 SuperMini (3.3 V logic, about 20–40 mA per pin at most and less is better) and a USB 5 V supply. Use those numbers in the examples.

Guides in this issue (slugs may be adjusted):

- `bjt-transistor-switch` — an NPN transistor (2N2222 / 2N3904) as a switch from a 3.3 V pin: base resistor, collector load, saturation. Demo: a circuit with a pin toggle and sliders for base resistor and load current; shows base current, required gain, whether it saturates, and Vce, with the transistor's pins labelled (E-B-C for TO-92).
- `mosfet-switch` — a logic-level N-channel MOSFET (e.g. AO3400, IRLZ44N) as a low-side switch: gate resistor, gate pull-down, why a non-logic-level part (IRFZ44N) barely turns on at 3.3 V. Demo: pick a part, toggle the gate, see Rds(on) at that Vgs, the heat in the MOSFET at a chosen load current, and the load on or off.
- `flyback-diodes` — switching a motor, relay or solenoid: why the coil kicks back and where the diode goes. Demo: a switch-off animation with the diode in, out, or reversed, plotting the voltage spike at the transistor (a teaching model, labelled as such).
- `bjt-vs-mosfet` — which to choose: a short comparison with a picker (load current, supply voltage, switching from 3.3 V) that recommends a part type and shows why.

### Assumptions
- All datasheet figures above are from memory; no datasheet was fetched this session. They are the commonly quoted values for these parts.
- Marked as estimates in `transistors.ts` (`estimate: true`) and starred in the demo: AO3400A at 3.3 V (40 mΩ, interpolated between the 2.5 V and 4.5 V points) and at 5 V (31 mΩ); IRLZ44N at 3.3 V (50 mΩ, up to about 10 A, from the typical output curves); IRFZ44N at 5 V (100 mΩ, about 3 A) and at 3.3 V (about 50 mA: "barely on"). Real parts vary widely near threshold.
- Vce(sat) end points not in the datasheet tables (1 mA for both parts, 200 mA for the 2N3904, 600 mA for the PN2222A) are extrapolated. Ic max for the PN2222A is taken as 600 mA (some makers rate 1 A).
- AO3400A RθJA 125 °C/W (steady state on a small pad). Junction temperatures assume 25 °C ambient and no heatsink.
- Vbe is a flat 0.7 V. The "linear" BJT case uses minimum hFE, so it's pessimistic.
- Flyback: coil inductances (relay 50 mH, motor 2 mH, solenoid 100 mH), coil resistances (relay 70 Ω, motor 20 Ω, solenoid 24 Ω) and a 100 pF stray capacitance (which sets the unclamped spike, I·√(L/C)) are teaching guesses, said so on the page. The no-clamp trace is drawn as a spike that dies within the first few percent of the window; it is not a simulation.
- Pinouts: 2N3904 and PN2222A as E-B-C (flat face toward you, legs down); P2N2222A as C-B-E. The page tells readers to check their maker's datasheet.
- The picker's thresholds (10 mA comfortable base current, 20 mA the most we'd take from a pin, MOSFET Vds at 80% of rating, 125 °C junction limit) are design rules of thumb chosen for beginners, not standards.
