## The part worth reading — a resolved issue's verdict has expired

`#0301` is **resolved**, and it judged `docs/deployment.md`'s `us-west-2`
occurrences **"correctly scoped"** — that is, deliberately left as a frozen
pre-deploy record.

`#0414`'s implementation pass argues that verdict no longer holds: the section
is stale enough now, and describes an SES setup that was never followed, that
"correctly scoped" reads as an endorsement of content a reader could act on.

**This is a claim about a closed decision, so establish it before acting.** Read
`#0301` and decide whether its reasoning has genuinely expired or whether the
section is still legitimately historical. Either answer is fine; asserting the
wrong one is not. `#0271`'s `## Scope` is the precedent for what "legitimately
historical" means, and it is a real category — not every stale string is a
defect.

## The distinction to apply

- **Historical record** — an account of what was true or planned when written,
  which a reader will not mistake for current instruction. Leave it, per
  `#0271`.
- **Live instruction or maintained reference** — something a reader would follow
  or copy. Correct it in place with a dated note, per `#0072`.

`docs/deployment.md`'s section is the hard case precisely because it is a
runbook — the genre a reader follows — that also self-declares as not-followed.
Say which it is and why.

5. **No mechanism** (`#0330`/`#0347`/`#0356`/`#0381`).
6. Editing `PRD.md` means running `./internal/db/...`, and **§11's section-index

   that ran. No Go-file-plus-line-number citation (`#0352`), scanned with
