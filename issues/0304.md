## Description

`#0281`'s guard flags any unscoped `ClaimDue`/`OrphanSweep` call outside
`internal/outbox`, and fails closed below **5 call sites found** against 32
measured. `#0281`'s review verified that floor is honoured and that the
name-collision handling is structurally sound.

**But 32 counts every call site, and 20 of them are inside `internal/outbox`,
where calls are exempt.** Only **12** are outside the package — the population
the guard actually checks. So the floor proves the scan reached the *exempt*
rows, not the *checked* ones.

Measured by `#0281`'s reviewer: narrowing `claimKindsGuardScanRoots` to
`[]string{"."}` leaves **23 sites (above the floor of 5), zero of them
evaluated, and the guard reports PASS.**

This is the same population-mismatch shape `#0275` criterion 4a closed for the
sibling guards, where a floor counted 247 walked files against 110 parsed. It is
not a bounce on `#0281`: criterion 4 as written is met, the constant's own
comment admits the limit, and CLAUDE.md §8 warns against stacking further
in-file checks without thought.

## Root cause

`claimKindsGuardMinPlausibleCallSiteCount` (5) is a floor on the TOTAL
call-site count — every `ClaimDue`/`OrphanSweep` mention the walk finds,
`inOwnPkg` or not. `internal/outbox`'s own `store_test.go` alone supplies ~20
of the 32 sites that floor is measured against, so a scan-roots regression
that keeps this package's own tree but drops every real caller outside it
still clears 5 without ever reaching the population the `VIOLATION` loop
actually evaluates (`!site.inOwnPkg`). Measured directly: narrowing
`claimKindsGuardScanRoots` to `[]string{"."}` finds 20 sites, all
`inOwnPkg`, `nonExemptCount == 0`, and the old guard reports PASS with
zero violations checked.

## Fix

Added a second floor, `claimKindsGuardMinPlausibleNonExemptCallSiteCount`
(6), applied to the non-exempt subset only (`!site.inOwnPkg`) —
`TestNoUnscopedOutboxClaimCallOutsidePackage` now fails closed on that
count independently of the existing total floor. Each const's doc comment
states which population it covers (criterion 2): the original proves the
walk reached *some* tree with real call sites; the new one proves it
reached the population the `VIOLATION` check evaluates. 6 was chosen over
the reviewer's suggested 5 so it also clears "at least half of today's 12"
(the margin `#0300`'s file-count floors use) while staying comfortably
above the worst case a single-file narrowing could produce (3, in
`internal/mailing/worker_store_test.go`).

Added `TestNonExemptFloorCatchesScanRootsNarrowedToSelf`, a **standing**
regression test (not a one-off manual proof) that walks the real repo tree
with `roots=[]string{"."}` via the guard's own production functions
(`walkClaimKindsGuardFiles`, `findOutboxCallSitesInFile`) and asserts both
halves of the finding every time `go test` runs: the OLD floor alone would
have reported PASS under this narrowing (`len(allSites) >=
claimKindsGuardMinPlausibleCallSiteCount`), and the NEW floor correctly
rejects it (`nonExemptCount == 0 <
claimKindsGuardMinPlausibleNonExemptCallSiteCount`).

**Criterion 5 decision: folded into `#0300`'s external harness, not a new
script.** Both `claimKindsGuardMinPlausibleCallSiteCount` and the new
`claimKindsGuardMinPlausibleNonExemptCallSiteCount` are ordinary `const`s
in the file they guard — setting either to `0` leaves `internal/outbox`'s
own `go test` run green for the exact mathematical reason `#0300`'s header
comment gives for the three handlers floors (`got < 0` can never fire for
a real non-negative count). That is precisely the fail-open `#0300` exists
to close, and `scripts/go_file_visit_floor_guard_test.sh` already carries
every helper this needs (`extract_const`, `sha_of`), so a second script
would just duplicate them — the CLAUDE.md §8 lesson `#0300` itself cites.
Added a clearly-delimited new section to that script: `count_outbox_call_sites`
(a grep-based population — every `.ClaimDue(`/`.OrphanSweep(` occurrence,
optionally excluding `internal/outbox`) plus a dedicated `outbox_floor_plausible`
margin (`0 < floor <= population`, deliberately *not* reusing `floor_plausible`'s
`>= population/2` bound, which fits file-count populations in the hundreds,
not call-site populations an order of magnitude smaller and dominated by a
single file — explained in the section's own header comment). It mutates
private copies of `claim_kinds_call_site_guard_test.go` in the harness's
existing `$WORKDIR`, never the tracked file, and re-hashes it before/after
to prove that.

**What did NOT move into the harness**: the `{"."}`-narrowing proof
(criterion 3). That is a behavioral claim about what `go test` reports
under a roots mutation, which — unlike a floor-to-0 mutation — DOES change
what `go test` observes (the walk's real yield, not just the threshold it
is compared against), so `go test` is a valid, non-circular oracle for it.
It stays in Go, in `TestNonExemptFloorCatchesScanRootsNarrowedToSelf`, and
is proved permanently there rather than as a one-off shell mutation.

### The external-harness / in-package-test split: the reasoning holds

The implementer's rule — external harness for constants the subject can zero,
in-package test for mutations the subject actually observes — is correct, and it
generalises. But the load-bearing criterion is one level down from how it is
phrased, and the sharper form is worth carrying forward:

> **After the mutation, is the assertion still falsifiable by the thing it
> measures?**

A floor forced to `0` makes `got < floor` unfalsifiable for any non-negative
`got`: the in-process test then agrees with itself regardless of what the walk
found — CLAUDE.md §8's "a copy of the answer stored next to the question".
A *roots* mutation changes `got` itself, so the in-process assertion still
measures something the mutation did not also satisfy, and `go test` is a
legitimate, non-circular oracle. That is a checkable rule; "constants go
external, roots stay in Go" is a proxy that happens to coincide with it today.

Two refinements the next agent should apply rather than the proxy:

- It is about the **direction of the comparison**, not the kind of thing
  mutated. A *ceiling* constant (`got > max`) mutated upward is equally
  unfalsifiable and needs the external oracle; a floor mutated *upward* is
  perfectly observable in Go, because it fails.
- The two homes are not exclusive. A mutation can be observable in Go and still
  deserve an external pin, because the observing test lives in the file it
  guards and the same edit can delete it. Keeping both — as this commit does —
  is right.

### Measured: the harness section's own premise is half wrong (#0304-A)

The new section's header states that "Setting **either** to 0 in the tracked
file leaves `internal/outbox`'s own `go test` run green". Mutation-measured in
the worktree, roots left intact:

| Mutation | `go test ./internal/outbox` | harness verdict |
|---|---|---|
| non-exempt floor = 0 | **FAIL** — `TestNonExemptFloorCatchesScanRootsNarrowedToSelf:487 REGRESSION: nonExemptCount=0 unexpectedly satisfies ...=0` | IMPLAUSIBLE |
| non-exempt floor = 1 | `ok` | **PLAUSIBLE** |
| total floor = 0 | `ok` | IMPLAUSIBLE |
| total floor = 1 | `ok` | **PLAUSIBLE** |

So the new standing Go test *already* pins the non-exempt floor against `0`
(its `nonExemptCount >= floor` regression assertion becomes `0 >= 0`) — a good
accident, but it means the section's stated justification for including that
floor is not a fact. And neither oracle catches a floor of **1**: the external
harness's added protection for this family is exactly the single value `0`.

Not a bounce — the redundancy is harmless, `> 0` is the right bar for the total
floor (a wholly broken walk still trips `1`), and `1` still catches the
`{"."}` narrowing on the non-exempt side, since that drives the count to `0`.
But the comment should say what is true. Filed as a follow-up.

### grep vs AST population: the one that matters agrees exactly

Counted independently with a standalone `go/ast` walker replicating
`findOutboxCallSitesInFile`'s predicate (`CallExpr` → `SelectorExpr` named
`ClaimDue`/`OrphanSweep`), over the same roots:

| | grep (harness) | AST (guard) |
|---|---|---|
| total | 35 | 32 |
| **non-exempt** | **12** | **12** |

**The non-exempt population — the one `claimKindsGuardMinPlausibleNonExemptCallSiteCount`
is pinned against — agrees exactly.** All twelve sites match one-for-one and all
have `argc=3`.

The total disagrees by 3, and the three are exactly the miscount class this
review was asked to look for, all inside `internal/outbox`:

- `claim_kinds_call_site_guard_test.go:156` — a `.ClaimDue(` / `.OrphanSweep(`
  inside a **prose comment**;
- lines 347 and 376 — `_, _ = s.OrphanSweep(...)` inside the **raw-string
  fixtures** `fixtureSrc` and `scopedFixtureSrc`.

The AST correctly counts **zero** call sites in that file; grep counts three.
The error direction is inflation, which only loosens the `floor <= population`
upper bound, so nothing is wrong today. Two things to know about it: the Go doc
comment says 32 and the harness measures 35 for what both call the same
population, and `grep -c` counts *lines*, so it will under-count if two guarded
calls ever share a line. Filed as a follow-up; the grep's independence from
`go/ast` is deliberate and correct per §8 and should not be "fixed" by making
the oracle parse Go.

### The weaker margin rule: defensible, but justified on the wrong number

`outbox_floor_plausible`'s `0 < floor <= population` (rather than the file-count
family's additional `floor >= population/2`) is the right call here, but its
in-comment justification cites a number that contradicts it: it says a call-site
population "CAN be dominated by one file (worst case 3, in
`internal/mailing/worker_store_test.go`)" — 3 of 12 is 25%, not dominated. That
claim is true of the **total** population (`store_test.go` supplies 20 of 35) and
false of the **non-exempt** one.

The real justification is stronger and unstated: `6` is *exactly* `12/2`, so
reusing `floor_plausible` would pass today and trip after **two** new non-exempt
call sites — a growth ceiling two ordinary commits away, against ~44 files of
headroom for the handlers family. That is why the weaker rule earns its place.

### Does a floor of 6 catch a realistic narrowing? Partly — measured

Against today's distribution of the 12 non-exempt sites (handlers 5, mailing 7):

| Narrowing | non-exempt left | caught by floor 6? |
|---|---|---|
| roots → `{"."}` (#0304's own case) | 0 | yes |
| roots → `{"../../cmd"}` | 0 | yes |
| roots → `internal/handlers` only | 5 | yes |
| roots → `internal/mailing` only | 7 | **no** |
| worst single-file narrowing | 3 | yes |

So `6` sits one short of catching a package-granularity narrowing to
`internal/mailing` — and that narrowing drops
`internal/handlers/subscribe_intake.go`, the production caller this guard most
exists for. A floor of `8` would catch every package-granularity narrowing
(`8 > 7`) while staying under the population (`8 <= 12`). Not a bounce:
criterion 1 explicitly left the number to the implementer to pick and justify,
and 6 exceeds the reviewer's own suggested 5 and is justified in-comment. Filed
as a follow-up.
