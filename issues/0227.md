## Description

`#0061`'s dashboard warns when the account-wide complaint rate crosses **0.3%**,
which is what its acceptance criterion specified and what `PRD.md` §11 records
as the Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender threshold. Its phase-3 review verified the
arithmetic exactly — 1/333 warns, 1/334 does not — and then noticed the number
may be the wrong one to warn on.

**`PRD.md` §6.9 records that AWS enforces 0.1% account-wide, and that crossing
it re-sandboxes the account.** So a rate of 0.2% sits inside AWS-review
territory with **no warning on the screen whose entire job is that warning** —
and the consequence there is not degraded inbox placement, it is sending
stopping altogether.

The implementation is correct against its criterion. What needs deciding is
whether the criterion is right. Two bands is the obvious answer — an amber at
0.1% ("AWS may review this account") and a red at 0.3% ("Gmail and Yahoo will
start filtering") — but that is a product call, not a bug fix.

**Three smaller items from the same review**, none blocking:

1. **Two comments cite a constant that does not exist.** `admin_dashboard.go:50`
   and `campaign_stats.go:133` both point at `send_health_complaint_pct` "in
   `worker.go` at 0.1". It lives nowhere — PRD §6.9's circuit breaker is not
   implemented, and `#0124` is the issue that would build it. The citations
   should point there. (This is the citation-drift class `#0220` is open about.)
2. **`AccountComplaintRate`'s numerator lacks the denominator's
   `s.status='sent'` filter.** Unreachable today, since only `MarkSent` writes a
   `ses_message_id` — but a rate above 100% is representable if that ever
   changes, and `AND s.status='sent'` costs nothing.
3. **Copy nit:** "SES is still in sandbox mode" is stated as observed fact for
   what is an operator-set environment flag with no live AWS call behind it. The
   default is the harmless direction, so this is polish.

## Decision

**Two bands: amber at 0.1%, red at 0.3%.** Recorded by the orchestrator on
2026-08-24 under the user's standing instruction to resolve Phase 7 rather than
stall on open questions.

The asymmetry of consequences decides it. Crossing Gmail/Yahoo's 0.3% degrades
inbox placement — bad, gradual, recoverable by sending better mail. Crossing
AWS's 0.1% re-sandboxes the account, and sending stops **altogether**. A screen
whose entire job is to warn about deliverability must not be silent through the
band where the more severe failure becomes possible. A 0.2% rate showing no
warning at all is the specific defect this issue names.

The counter-argument — a quieter dashboard — does not survive the comparison:
an amber that occasionally reads as noise costs attention, while a missing
warning costs the ability to send.

**Required wording distinction**, since the two bands mean genuinely different
things and a single escalating colour would blur them:

| Band | Threshold | Says |
|---|---|---|
| Amber | ≥ 0.1% | AWS may review this account and return it to the sandbox — sending would stop |
| Red | ≥ 0.3% | Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender limits exceeded — mail will be filtered |

Both boundaries get the treatment `#0061`'s review gave 0.3%: one send either
side, asserting the band flips. Note the amber boundary is the arithmetically
harder one — 1/1000 warns, 1/1001 does not — so it is the one most worth
proving rather than assuming.

**Not in scope here:** actually enforcing either threshold. The circuit breaker
that would stop sending is `#0124`, unimplemented. This issue makes the
dashboard tell the truth; it does not make the system act on it.
