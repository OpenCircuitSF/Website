## Carried in from `#0014`'s review (2026-08-18, updated at the fourth pass)

- [ ] **Reserve final height before paint, or re-apply the scroll offset once
      the fetch settles.** *(Narrowed — the router no longer restores after a
      single `await tick()`.)* Since `243abad` the restore re-applies across
      animation frames for up to `RESTORE_DEADLINE_MS` (1 s, `router.ts`),
      bailing out early if the user scrolls. A `/workshops/:slug` fetch that
      settles inside that window therefore needs **no work from this issue at
      all**. What still bites: a fetch slower than 1 s, or a view that keeps
      growing *after* the deadline (lazily-loaded images with no reserved box,
      late web fonts, content appended by a second request). In those cases the
      loop gives up against a document that has not finished growing and Back
      silently lands short. Reserving the final height before paint is the
      robust fix; re-applying the offset once your own fetch settles is the
      cheap one.
- [ ] **Do not leave the page while its restore is still clamped.** See
      [#0074](0074.md) — the fix belongs in `router.ts`, not here, but this is
      the first route on which it becomes routine.

- [ ] **Watch the one-animation-frame stamp window.** `#0074`'s reviewer measured
      it: the divergence bail-out runs on the next rAF, so a save dispatched
      within ~8 ms of a real scroll consumes `pendingRestoreTarget` before
      `finish()` clears it. **Not a regression** — the pre-`#0074` build stamped
      the clamped value in that same window and Back lands identically on both,
      because the recorded number is always the entry's own prior value. But a
      progressively growing document could make the difference visible here. If
      it does, the cheap fix is clearing the target from `onScroll` on divergence
      rather than waiting a frame.

> The third review also warned that the retry loop was *hostile* on this route —
> a nav click during a workshop-detail restore scrolled the next page to the
> wrong offset. **That is fixed:** `f6b204e` cancels the in-flight restore at the
> top of `navigate()`, reproduced and re-verified at the fourth review. Ignore
> that half of the older note.
