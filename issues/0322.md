## Description

`#0304` set `claimKindsGuardMinPlausibleNonExemptCallSiteCount = 6` against 12
non-exempt `ClaimDue`/`OrphanSweep` call sites. `#0304`'s review measured which
narrowings that floor actually catches:

| Narrowing | Sites left | Caught by floor 6? |
|---|---|---|
| `{"."}` | 0 | yes |
| `cmd` only | 0 | yes |
| `internal/handlers` only | 5 | yes |
| worst single file | 3 | yes |
| **`internal/mailing` only** | **7** | **no** |

**The one that evades it is the one that matters most.** A `mailing`-only
narrowing drops `internal/handlers/subscribe_intake.go` — the production caller
the guard most exists for, since `#0254`'s duplicate-send defect came from an
unscoped sweep on exactly that path.

A floor of **8** closes it (`8 > 7`, `8 <= 12`).

## The trade-off to weigh

`#0275`'s failure mode is the opposite one: a floor set **above** its population
makes the guard impossible to pass, and that issue had to lower a floor from 150
to 80 for precisely that reason.

At 8 against 12 there are 4 sites of shrink headroom. Note that **3 of the 12 are
`mailing.SendStore`'s unrelated same-named `OrphanSweep`** — a rename or a
refactor removing those would leave 9, still above 8 but with only 1 to spare.

So this is a judgement, not an arithmetic result. Make it deliberately.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Decide the floor and **record the package-granularity table above** as its
   justification, re-measured rather than copied from this issue.
2. Whatever number is chosen, state in the comment which narrowings it catches
   and which it does not. A floor that silently fails to catch the
   `subscribe_intake.go`-dropping case must say so.
3. Weigh the `#0275` failure mode explicitly: how much shrink headroom remains,
   and what happens if `mailing.SendStore`'s `OrphanSweep` is renamed.
4. If the answer is that a count-based floor cannot catch this case robustly,
   say so and propose what would — for instance asserting that specific known
   production call sites are present, which is a different and stronger check
   than counting.
5. `#0321` is open on the same section's comments; sequence the two rather than
   editing the same lines twice.
6. `ISSUE=NNNN scripts/check.sh go ./internal/outbox/...` and
   `scripts/check.sh guards` pass, the latter run at least twice.

## Recommendation reported, not filed (CLAUDE.md §9)

**A count-based floor cannot robustly guarantee the specific
`subscribe_intake.go` call sites survive a narrowing.** Consider a stronger
guard that asserts specific known production call sites (starting with
`internal/handlers/subscribe_intake.go`'s two `ClaimDue`/`OrphanSweep`
calls) are present in the non-exempt population, rather than only counting
them. This is a presence check, not a count, so it cannot be defeated by a
narrowing that happens to leave enough OTHER non-exempt sites standing.
Rationale: acceptance criterion 4 above; not built here because it is a
change to the guard's shape, out of this issue's scope (the floor's value).

## Review notes

**Verdict: bounced.** The floor change itself is correct and was proved to work
— but acceptance criterion 3 is answered with a number that is wrong, and
wrong in the unsafe direction. Criterion 1 required that number be re-measured
rather than copied from this issue; it was copied, and this issue had it wrong.

### What is right, and was proved rather than argued

The central claim holds. Mutation-tested in a throwaway `git worktree` at HEAD,
with `claimKindsGuardScanRoots` narrowed to `{"../mailing"}` and an own
`opencircuit_test_0321`:

```
--- FAIL: TestNoUnscopedOutboxClaimCallOutsidePackage
    found only 7 ClaimDue/OrphanSweep call site(s) OUTSIDE internal/outbox
    under [../mailing] (of 7 total, …), want at least 8
```

A floor of 6 passes that narrowing; a floor of 8 fails it. The gap this issue
was filed for is genuinely closed.

The package-granularity table was re-measured from scratch with a standalone
`go/ast` program written for this review (not by calling the guard's own
functions): `internal/handlers` **5**, `internal/mailing` **7**, total
non-exempt **12**, every site `argc=3`. Worst single non-exempt file is
`internal/mailing/worker_store_test.go` at 3. `cmd` has none. Every row of the
committed table checks out, and `8 <= 12` holds.

Criterion 4 is met and met honestly: the comment states plainly that a count is
blind to *which* sites it counted, and names the presence-based alternative
without building it. `#0330` now carries that.

### The defect: it is 4 of 12, not 3, and that costs the last site of headroom

The doc comment states:

> At 8 against 12 there are 4 sites of headroom — but **3 of the 12** are
> `mailing.SendStore`'s unrelated same-named `OrphanSweep` … would drop the
> population to **9**, leaving only 1 site of headroom.

Measured: **four** of the twelve target `SendStore.OrphanSweep`, not three.

| Site | Receiver | Which `OrphanSweep` |
|---|---|---|
| `internal/mailing/worker.go:575` | `Worker.store`, declared `*SendStore` | **SendStore's** |
| `internal/mailing/worker_store_test.go:109` | `store := NewSendStore(…)` | **SendStore's** |
| `internal/mailing/worker_store_test.go:151` | same | **SendStore's** |
| `internal/mailing/worker_store_test.go:165` | same | **SendStore's** |

`worker.go:575` is `w.store.OrphanSweep(ctx, c.ID, orphanStaleAfter)` —
`c.ID` is a campaign id, matching `SendStore.OrphanSweep(ctx, campaignID
int64, staleAfter time.Duration)` at `internal/mailing/worker_store.go:297`.
It is not an `outbox.Store` call. (`internal/mailing/outbox_worker.go`'s two
calls *are* `outbox.Store`'s — `OutboxWorker.store` is `*outbox.Store`.)

So the answer criterion 3 asks for is:

- a rename of `SendStore.OrphanSweep` drops the non-exempt population to **8**,
  not 9;
- that leaves **zero** sites of headroom, not one — the floor would sit exactly
  *at* the population, and any further shrink makes the guard unsatisfiable;
- equivalently: **8 is exactly the number of genuine, non-collision outbox call
  sites outside the package.** The entire margin this floor currently enjoys is
  supplied by an unrelated method that merely shares a name.

That is a materially different answer to the `#0275` question than the one
recorded, and it is the difference between "one step from the `#0275` failure
mode" and "at it".

**The file already contradicts itself about this.** `nameMatchesGuardedMethod`'s
own doc comment, ~100 lines below the new text, has it right and always did:

> Verified directly, not assumed: grepped the whole tree … — `worker.go:543`
> **and** `worker_store_test.go`'s three `OrphanSweep` calls are the only
> non-outbox occurrences with `argc==3` …

Four sites, named. The new comment now asserts three. One of the two is wrong
and a future reader has no way to tell which.

The error did not originate here — `#0304`'s own comment attached the
"SendStore's unrelated" parenthetical to `worker_store_test.go` alone, `#0304`'s
review repeated it, and this issue's Description repeated it again. Nobody
re-measured. That is exactly the `#0310` shape criterion 1 was written to
prevent, and the sibling issue `#0321` criterion 4 spells out.

### The fix is real, but its margin in the growth direction is one caller, and it expires silently

Worth recording alongside the shrink analysis, because it is the same question
from the other side. Measured in the same worktree: with `claimKindsGuardScanRoots`
narrowed to `{"../mailing"}` and **one ordinary, correctly-scoped
`store.ClaimDue(ctx, 10, outbox.KindConfirmation)` added to
`internal/mailing`**, `internal/mailing` reaches 8 non-exempt sites and

```
ok  github.com/brennanMKE/OpenCircuitSF/internal/outbox  0.244s
```

The guard passes again, with `subscribe_intake.go` dropped and the `#0254`
caller unprotected. Nothing signals it: `go test` is green, and the harness is
green too, because the population merely grows to 13 and `0 < 8 <= 13` still
holds.

So `8` is valid only while `internal/mailing` holds exactly 7 non-exempt sites.
`internal/mailing` is the package most likely to gain outbox callers — it
already owns `OutboxWorker`. Compare the shrink direction, which is at least
*loud*: a rename fails `go test` immediately.

This is not a reason to reject 8 — it is strictly better than 6, which fails
today rather than after a hypothetical commit. It is a reason the comment
should say that the number is pinned to a distribution that can change without
anything noticing, which is a sharper statement of criterion 4's point than
"blind to which sites it counted".

### What to change

1. Correct the count to **4 of 12**, the post-rename population to **8**, and
   the remaining headroom to **0**. Re-measure it; do not copy it from these
   notes either.
2. Say that `8` equals the count of genuine non-collision outbox call sites
   outside the package, so the floor's whole margin comes from a name
   collision.
3. Reconcile with `nameMatchesGuardedMethod`'s doc comment rather than leaving
   two contradictory statements in one file. That comment's `worker.go:543` is
   also stale — the call is at `worker.go:575` today.
4. Record that the fix's validity in the *growth* direction is one
   `internal/mailing` caller wide and expires with no signal (the measurement
   above), and that only the shrink direction fails loudly.
5. Re-run `ISSUE=NNNN scripts/check.sh go ./internal/outbox/...` and the floor
   harness. **Do not lower the floor** — 8 is the right number; only its
   justification is wrong.

### Should this block on `#0330`?

**No.** `#0330`'s presence-based guard is the structural fix and should be built,
but it is a different change to a different mechanism, and this issue's own
criterion 4 explicitly asked only that the limit be stated and the alternative
proposed — which it does, well. Raising the floor is a real improvement on its
own terms, proved above. Fix the four points above and this can resolve without
waiting for `#0330`.
