## Description

A hard delete that removes a subscriber's personal data while keeping the address suppressed so it cannot silently return.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `DELETE /admin/subscribers/{id}` hard-deletes the subscriber and cascades `subscriber_interests`
- [ ] A `suppressions` row with reason `manual` is left in place so the address is not re-added
- [ ] `email_sends` rows are anonymized rather than deleted, preserving campaign counts
- [ ] `email_events` retains the raw payload for deliverability forensics but the linkage is severed
- [ ] The action requires a typed confirmation and is audited with the requester's identity
- [ ] A data-export-on-request path exists (reuse #0059 filtered to one subscriber)
- [ ] Privacy policy documents what is retained after erasure and why
- [ ] Tests confirm the subscriber is gone, the suppression remains, and counts are intact

## Notes

PRD §11. The tension here is real: erasure must be genuine, but keeping a suppression entry is what prevents the address being re-added by a later import or signup — which is itself a privacy protection. Documenting that in the privacy policy is what makes it defensible.

Anonymizing rather than deleting `email_sends` preserves the historical campaign metrics that would otherwise silently change.
