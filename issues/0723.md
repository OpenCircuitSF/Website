Facts must be checkable against raspberrypi.com/documentation and product briefs; anything from memory goes under `### Assumptions`.

Guides in this issue:

- `pi-find-on-network` — "Where's my Pi?" Finding a headless Pi: `hostname.local` (mDNS, Avahi on the Pi; works from macOS, Linux and current Windows), the router's DHCP client list, a network scan (`ping`, `arp -a`, a phone scanner app), and why it fails on guest networks with client isolation, captive-portal café WiFi and some corporate networks that block multicast; a phone hotspot as the fallback. Demo: pick a network type and the laptop's OS, and see which methods work and why.
- `pi-models-compared` — Zero 2W, Pi 4 or Pi 5: which for which job. Specs that matter (CPU, RAM options, WiFi bands, Ethernet, video ports, USB, power supply, size, idle power). Demo: tick what the project needs (battery, small size, Ethernet, 5 GHz WiFi, a desktop, two displays, audio DAC HAT, LED panel driving) and get a board with reasons.
- `pi-gpio-pinout` — the 40-pin header: physical pin numbers vs GPIO (BCM) numbers, 3.3 V logic that is **not 5 V tolerant**, the 5 V, 3.3 V and ground pins, I2C, SPI, UART and PWM pins, and per-pin current limits. Demo modelled on `esp32-c3-supermini-pinout`: tap a pin to see what it does; toggle the label scheme between physical and GPIO numbers; a warning on the 5 V pins.
