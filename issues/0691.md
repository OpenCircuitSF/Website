Facts used (standard references):
- EIA 3-digit capacitor code: two significant digits, then a multiplier digit (0–6 = ×10ⁿ, 8 = ×0.01, 9 = ×0.1), in pF. So 104 = 100 nF and 472 = 4.7 nF. Tolerance letters are B/C/D (pF), F ±1%, G ±2%, J ±5%, K ±10%, M ±20%, Z +80/−20%. "R" marks the decimal point (4R7 = 4.7 pF).
- RC: V(t) = V_target + (V0 − V_target)·e^(−t/RC). That is 63.2% at τ and 99.3% at 5τ.
- An aluminium electrolytic's stripe marks the negative lead (the shorter lead on a new part). A tantalum's band or "+" marks the positive lead.
- ESP32-C3 supply range 3.0–3.6 V (the datasheet's recommended operating VDD); 3.0 V is the scope's limit line.

### Assumptions
- **The decoupling demo is a teaching model**, and is labelled so on the page. The numbers are chosen to teach, not measured: 25 nH and 0.2 Ω of supply wiring, a 100 mA spike rising in 5 ns and lasting 20 ns, 1 nH of capacitor body inductance, about 1 nH per mm of trace. The spike sees the supply inductance in parallel with the capacitor path. The charge sag is I·t/C, there is no ringing, and the overshoot on the falling edge is drawn but clamped on the plot. Only the trend is meant to be right: no capacitor is worst, close is better than far, and 10 nF runs out of charge.
- Voltage-rating rule of thumb: "ok" at ≥ 1.5× the rail, "tight" above the rail but below that, "over" below the rail. This is a common maker guideline, not a standard. The guide text says "10 V or higher on 5 V; 6.3 V works but has little margin".
- These come from memory, not a page fetched this session:
  - LED strip bulk capacitor of about 1000 µF across the strip's power input. This is the common advice in Adafruit's NeoPixel guide, from memory. The guide says "rated 10 V for a 5 V strip".
  - The SuperMini already carries decoupling capacitors.
  - Ceramics range "from a few pF up to a few µF" (through-hole and the usual parts; SMD MLCCs reach tens of µF).
  - A button debounce example of 10 kΩ × 100 nF, which gives τ = 1 ms.
- The RC demo uses ideal parts. The note says real capacitors leak and electrolytics can be about 20% off their marked value (a typical ±20% tolerance).

## Review notes

Verdict: **resolved** after small fixes made in review (`9ed002a9`).

Checked every fact a reader sees. EIA decoding (0–6 add zeros, 8 = ×0.01, 9 = ×0.1, 7 unused), R and unit-letter decimals, and the tolerance letters B/C/D/F/G/J/K/M/Z are all correct. So are the polarity rules (electrolytic stripe = −, tantalum band/+ = +), τ = RC, the 63.2/86.5/95.0/99.3% points, 10 kΩ × 100 µF = 1 s, 1 kΩ × 10 µF = 10 ms, and ESP32-C3 VDD 3.0–3.6 V. The decoupling model's closed form (L·di/dt plus I·t/C plus I·R) gives 2.80 V with no capacitor, 3.24 V for 100 nF at 2 mm, and 3.08 V for 10 nF at 1 mm. It is labelled as a teaching model.

Fixed in review:
- `105K` (1 µF) drew an electrolytic can. A three-digit code is never printed on a can, so codes now always draw a disc.
- On the polarity SVG, the "marked lead is shorter" trick drew the longer lead with a visible gap under the can, and a stub on the other side. The leads are now plain lines into the body.
- The rail SVG's label named a "marked lead" for a ceramic. It now says it has no polarity.
- The decoupling verdict said "sits right at the pin" for a 100 nF part 10–33 mm away, still above 3.0 V. It now names the trace inductance and says to move the capacitor closer.
- RC text: "pick R and C so that 5τ is about the delay you want" was wrong for a debounce or reset, where the input crosses its threshold at about 1τ. It now separates the threshold (about 1τ) from settled (5τ).
- The smoke tests now cover 105K → disc, 10µF 16V → can, and the 30 mm "Holding, but close" verdict.

Verification, re-run by the reviewer: `ISSUE=0691 scripts/check.sh` gave VERIFICATION PASSED, with 142 files and 5507 vitest tests and svelte-check clean. Load was 11 at the start, from another worktree's full Go suite. `ISSUE=0691 scripts/check.sh go ./internal/guides/... ./internal/seo/... ./cmd/opencircuit/...` reported all three `ok` with no skips.
